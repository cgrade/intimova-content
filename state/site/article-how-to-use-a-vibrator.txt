Sex Toys & Buying Guide How to Use a Vibrator for the First Time (No Awkward Guessing)
New to it and not sure where to start? Here’s how to use a vibrator, step by step — what you need, where to put it, settings, and how to clean it afterwards.

If you’ve just got your first one — or you’re about to — here’s exactly how to use a vibrator , without the awkward guessing. The honest truth is there’s very little to learn: the toy does the work, and curiosity is the only skill required.
The short version: charge it, get comfortable, add a little lubricant, start on the lowest setting, and explore the outside first. Now the detail.
What you’ll need
A charged vibrator. A rechargeable bullet or an air-pulse rose are the easiest first toys.
Water-based lubricant. A little makes everything better and is safe with silicone toys .
Privacy and no rush. A locked door and time to yourself. That’s the whole setup.
How to use a vibrator, step by step
Get comfortable. Lie back somewhere you feel relaxed and unhurried.
Add a little lubricant. A small amount improves comfort and sensation.
Start on the lowest setting. Always begin gentle — you can build up.
Explore the outside first. Rest it around and on the clitoris and vulva, where most women feel the most. Move it slowly to find what feels good.
Adjust speed, pressure and angle. Small changes make a big difference. Follow what feels good; there’s no "right" way.
Take your time. There’s no target and no rush. Let the outcome look after itself.
External vs internal
Most vibrators — and most pleasure — are about external, clitoral stimulation, which is where the majority of women reach orgasm (more on that in why most women don’t orgasm during sex ). Some vibrators can be used internally too, but you don’t need to — start outside, and only explore internal sensation if you’re curious.
Gentle troubleshooting
Feels like too much? Drop a setting, or move slightly off-centre.
Feels like nothing? Reposition, add more lubricant, or try a different angle.
Oversensitive afterward? Completely normal — pause, and sensation returns.
Cleaning and care
Rinse the toy with warm water and a mild, fragrance-free toy cleaner after each use, dry it, and store it somewhere clean and private. Body-safe silicone is non-porous and easy to keep hygienic — see how to choose a body-safe sex toy .
