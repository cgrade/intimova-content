Sex Tips & Techniques How to Last Longer in Bed: Techniques That Actually Work
Finishing sooner than you’d like is common and fixable. Here’s how to last longer in bed — real techniques, the mindset that helps, and the aids worth trying.

If you finish sooner than you’d like, here’s the reassuring truth first: it’s one of the most common experiences there is, it’s rarely a medical problem, and how to last longer in bed is a skill you can genuinely build. This is the practical, no-shame guide.
The short version: slow down, take the pressure off penetration, train a few simple techniques, and relax. Aids can help, but technique is what lasts.
Why it happens
Finishing quickly usually comes down to arousal and nerves , not a flaw. Over-excitement, anxiety, being out of practice, or simply rushing all play a part. The good news: all of those respond to practice and a calmer approach.
Techniques that actually work
These are the proven ones. They work best practised over a few weeks — think training, not a one-night fix.
Start-stop. As you get close, stop all stimulation until the urge fades, then start again. Repeating this builds control.
The squeeze. Near the point of no return, a firm squeeze just below the head of the penis dials the urge back down.
Edging. Deliberately approaching the edge and backing off — solo or with a partner — trains your body to hold there longer.
Kegels (pelvic-floor training). The same muscles that control finishing can be strengthened. Squeeze as if stopping your urine mid-flow, hold a few seconds, release. A set daily builds real control.
Slow your breathing. Fast, shallow breathing rushes you; slow breaths keep you in control.
Take the pressure off penetration
A huge amount of "lasting longer" is really about not making penetration the whole event . Spend more time on foreplay, use your hands and mouth, and bring in a toy — when your partner’s pleasure isn’t riding on how long you last, the pressure (and the finishing-too-fast) eases. This also helps her : most women need clitoral stimulation to orgasm anyway (see why most women don’t orgasm during sex ).

The mindset
Confidence is half the battle. The more relaxed and present you are — and the less you treat sex as a performance to pass — the longer you’ll naturally last. Pressure is the enemy; slowing down is the fix.
When to see a doctor
If it’s new, persistent and genuinely distressing, or comes with other symptoms, see a doctor — the cause is often straightforward.
The bottom line
Learning to last longer in bed is about slowing down, training a few techniques, taking the pressure off penetration, and relaxing. Aids can help around the edges, but the skills are what change things for good. A private WhatsApp chat is always open if you’d like guidance.
