Desire & Libido How to Talk to Your Partner About Sex Toys
Suggesting a toy can feel more nerve-wracking than buying one. Here’s how to talk to your partner about sex toys — what to say, when, and how to keep it about connection, not replacement.

Suggesting a toy to a partner can feel more nerve-wracking than buying one for yourself. If you want to know how to talk to your partner about sex toys without it landing wrong, the whole thing comes down to one reframe — and a bit of timing.
The worry underneath it is almost always the same: will they think they’re not enough? So let’s start there, because naming it out loud takes most of the sting out of the conversation before it begins.
First, the reframe: a toy is not a replacement
A toy is an accessory to connection — a way to add sensation, playfulness and curiosity to something you already share. This isn’t wishful thinking, either: couples who use toys together tend to report more satisfaction and better communication, not less intimacy. The toy isn’t competition. It’s a shared idea you get to explore on the same team.
Say that part plainly and early, and the rest of the conversation gets far easier.
Pick a calm, clothed moment
The worst time to raise it is in the middle of intimacy, when either of you might read it as a critique. Choose a relaxed, low-stakes moment — a walk, a quiet evening in, a long drive — where it’s clearly a curious idea, not a complaint. Neutral ground, clothes on, no pressure to act on anything tonight.
Lead with "us", not "you"
How you open the sentence matters more than the words themselves. Frame it as something you’re curious about together :
"I read something about couples trying a toy together and it made me curious — would you be up for exploring that with me?"
"I love what we have. I’d love to add a little play to it. Can I show you something I found?"
"There’s no pressure at all — I just think it could be fun for both of us."
Notice these are invitations, not verdicts. You’re opening a door, not delivering a review of their performance. Then listen more than you pitch.
If they hesitate, that’s normal
A pause isn’t a no. If your partner seems unsure, resist the urge to sell harder — get curious instead. "What’s the hesitation? Talk to me." Common worries and honest answers:
"Do you not enjoy sex with me?" — "I do. This is about adding to it, not fixing it."
"Isn’t that a bit much?" — "We start as small and simple as you like. There’s no deep end."
"What if I feel silly?" — "We both might, and that’s part of the fun. We can laugh."
You’re not trying to win the conversation in one sitting. Sometimes the right answer is "no rush — just sit with it."
What to suggest first
If you’re both open to it, begin with something designed for connection rather than intensity. A discreet rechargeable bullet is small, simple and easy to introduce together. A dual toy like the Rose Duo offers shared sensation without complexity, and a wand is great if you both like stronger, external stimulation. Keep the first time light and unhurried — plenty of water-based lubricant , low settings, and room to laugh.
New to toys entirely? How to use a rose toy and choosing your first toy are gentle places to start together.
Keep talking afterwards
The conversation doesn’t end when the toy arrives. Check in: what did you both enjoy, what would you change? That ongoing, honest dialogue is the intimacy — the toy is just a good reason to have it.
